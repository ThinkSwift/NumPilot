How it works

AI composes. The solver computes.

Most “AI physics” tools ask a language model for the answer. NumPilot never does — the AI only sets up the problem, and a deterministic numerical solver does the actual physics.

1

Describe an analysis

Say it the way you'd say it to a colleague. No mesh, no boundary-condition menus, no manual.

✨ how hot a 50 W chip runs on a copper spreader
a comb drive with a tighter gap resistance of a thin copper wire a thick comb actuator at 80 V
2

AI picks a verified template

The AI maps your words to one of 14 verified templates and sets parameters — every value clamped to the solver's validated range. If your ask doesn't fit, it says so instead of faking it.

source6 mm ✓ in bounds

Your iPhone does the physics

A finite-difference / finite-element solve runs entirely on device — matrix-free preconditioned conjugate gradient, converged in a blink. Then drag sliders and it re-solves instantly.

The AI never invents numbers. It can only choose a verified template and set parameters inside validated bounds. Every result on screen came out of a deterministic solver — and the AI's one-line summary is grounded only in those computed numbers.

Run · tweak · share

From prompt to publishable report.

🎚️

Live sliders, instant re-solve

Every model opens as an interactive studio. Drag a gap, a voltage, a fin count — the solver re-runs the full physics in real time, and the field visualization follows.

📄

One-tap technical reports

Export a clean LaTeX + PDF report — governing equations, parameter and result tables, and the field figure. Ready for a lab notebook, a design review, or a homework hand-in.

🔗

Share it as a tiny link

A model travels as a recipe, not a screenshot: numpilot.com/m?r=… Anyone who opens it gets the exact same model — re-solved on their own device — ready to tweak, fork, and publish to the community board.
